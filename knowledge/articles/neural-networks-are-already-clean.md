An informal Goodfire research essay (about 65 minutes to read, published 2026-10-15) arguing that mechanistic interpretability is in much better shape than the field believed. Cammarata argues that the early model-organism evidence for polysemanticity and superposition was misread: what looked like neurons packing unrelated concepts was mostly a motif he calls recursive generalization, plus several ordinary and mostly fixable issues. If so, networks can be studied directly instead of first being untangled. He reports that with modern agentic tools most neurons in models up to trillions of parameters are legible, in contrast to the roughly 2% he found legible in OpenAI's first large-scale automated interpretability project.

Note: only the abstract and "Short Version" were captured; the body of the essay was not available.

## Key Takeaways

- Cause of polysemanticity in early model organisms was not superposition but ordinary, mostly fixable issues.
- Apparent arbitrary packing was largely "recursive generalization", which is much less concerning.
- Most neurons in models up to trillions of parameters appear legible, and their circuits clean.
- Implication: ambitious interpretability may be solved by the time it is needed, as automated scientists improve ("bitter lesson" framing: the simple thing at absurd scale works).
- Essay structure: revisiting canonical evidence, vision models, language models, zooming out to whole networks.
